Why Cravings Arrive Early

Predictive Hunger & Sugar Signals

A craving can start before the food is in front of you. Often it starts when the body predicts food is coming. Hunger hormones can rise before your usual meal time.

The Science

  • The hypothalamus runs the body's hunger and satiety control.
  • Ghrelin from the gut can rise around learned meal times and increase the desire to eat.
  • Sugar sensing in the gut can drive dopamine linked signals that encourage more sugar seeking.

The Protocol

  • Shift a learned snack cue gradually instead of all at once.
  • Put fiber rich food before the sweet item when you can.
  • Watch timing, place, smell, and routine as craving cues.

See it in real life

Example

The craving starts two blocks early

Ivan has $171 in an envelope taped under his desk drawer, and a guy two streets over will hold a $240 used mountain bike for him until the end of the month. Every school day, Ivan spends $4.29 at the gas station by his bus stop: a large blue slushie and a pack of sour worms.

The strange part is when the want starts: at 3:05, when the bus turns past the car wash, two blocks before his stop. By the time the doors open at 3:12, his mouth is watering. On Saturdays, mowing lawns at 3:12, he never thinks about slushies.

His body learned the schedule. Ghrelin, a hunger hormone from the gut, can rise around the times he usually eats, so his body gets ready before he chooses anything. The car wash, the clock and the smell of the store are the cue. Sugar also signals the brain from the gut, which is part of why one sweet thing tends to ask for another. And lunch was at 10:50, so some of it is plain hunger.

He doesn’t try to win on willpower. He packs an apple and a peanut butter sandwich and eats them on the bus before the car wash. He rides one more stop and walks home past the park. The sweet thing moves home and a little later: two of his mom’s cookies at 3:30, after the sandwich, and the next week 3:45.

The first few days the pull still hits at the car wash and fades once the bus passes the store. On the second Thursday he doesn’t notice the store until the bus is a block past it. On the 29th the envelope holds $248, and he rides the bike home.

  1. Where it stalls$4.29 a day, and the want starts two blocks before the store
  2. What changes itApple and sandwich on the bus, one more stop, sweets later
  3. What happens nextA block past the store before he notices, then the bike

Try it yourself · 2 minutes

The lemon test

No food needed. Sit somewhere you can close your eyes for a minute.

  1. Close your eyes and picture a lemon on a cutting board: the bright yellow skin, the knife going through, the sharp smell when it splits.
  2. Picture squeezing one half so the juice runs over your fingers. Then picture biting into the other half.
  3. Stop and notice what your mouth is doing.
  4. Now picture the snack you usually reach for at a set time: the clock, the room, the sound of the package opening. Notice whether a pull starts before any food is there.

What you’ll probably notice. There was no lemon anywhere, and your mouth likely watered anyway. That’s your body getting ready to eat because of a prediction. Cravings run on the same kind of prediction: the clock, the room and the smell can start the preparation before you’ve decided anything. That’s useful to know, because a cue is something you can move.

Step by step

Move the cue a little at a time

Nothing here asks you to skip food. It’s about when and where the craving starts.

  1. Catch the cue. For a few days, when a craving hits, jot down the time, the place, any smell and what you were doing. Look for the one that repeats.
  2. Eat something with fiber first. Before the sweet thing, have real food, like an apple, a sandwich, carrots and hummus, or a handful of nuts. Then decide what else you want.
  3. Move it 10 or 15 minutes later. Shift the snack time a little every few days instead of an hour all at once.
  4. Change the routine around the cue. A different route, a different seat, one more stop. It’s often easier to change the setting than to argue with a craving in the middle of it.
  5. Give the wave a few minutes. When a predicted craving shows up, wait a few minutes. It often eases once the cue has passed. If you’re hungry, eat.

How to tell it’s working. Keep your cue notes for a week. If the craving starts showing up at the new time or place instead of the old one, the prediction is retraining.

When it gets hard. If you tried moving it an hour or dropping it overnight and it snapped back, that’s the reason to go small. Shift it 10 minutes and keep the real food first.

Good to know. If thoughts about food fill a lot of your day, or eating starts to feel out of your control, that’s more than a snack schedule can handle. Tell a parent and ask to see your doctor.
