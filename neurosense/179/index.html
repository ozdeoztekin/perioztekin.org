Surf the Urge

urge surfing cravings

An urge feels like a command because it arrives with real body volume.

The Science

  • A craving moves like a wave of prediction that can rise, crest, and pass.
  • Your nervous system expects relief and pushes the action that worked before.
  • Watching the urge can change how you respond to it, even when the feeling stays loud.

The Protocol

  • Locate the urge in your body without acting, for one minute.
  • Describe the wave as rising, peaking, shifting, or fading.
  • Choose your next action after the peak, once the body volume has dropped.

See it in real life

Example

One minute with her hand off the mouse

The defeat screen fades back to the menu, and the menu music starts its loop again. That’s three losses in a row, and the badge next to Tamar’s name says Gold 2 now. Getting to Gold 1 took her a month.

It’s twenty to eleven, and her own rule is no ranked games after ten. Her cursor is already on Find Match, because one more game could let the night end on a win. The last time she chased a losing streak, she played until after one and dropped a whole tier. She knows that. Her hand is still on the mouse, and she keeps telling herself not to click.

An urge moves like a wave. It rises, crests and passes. Her nervous system expects relief and pushes the action that brought relief before, and one more match has rescued a bad night often enough that the urge lands like an order. Arguing with every feeling can keep her attention glued to it, while watching it can change how she responds to it, even when the feeling stays loud.

She takes her hand off the mouse and lays it flat on the desk, and for one minute she doesn’t act. She finds where the urge lives, in the heat in her face, her clamped jaw and a buzz in her right palm. Then she describes the wave in her head as it moves. It rises when the menu music loops, peaks when she thinks about the lost points, shifts down into her stomach and starts to thin out.

When the buzz has left her hand, she chooses. She closes the game, texts her duo partner, Cyra, “done for tonight, tomorrow after practice?” and hangs her headset on its hook. She’s asleep before midnight, and in the morning Gold 2 is still Gold 2.

  2. What changes itShe lays her hand flat on the desk and watches the wave for one minute.
  3. What happens nextShe closes the game after the peak, falls asleep and keeps her rank.

Try it yourself · 2 minutes

Watch an urge pass

You can watch small urges rise and fall right now. Sit somewhere comfortable and keep this page open for the counter below.

  1. Sit still for two minutes with your hands resting, without scratching, shifting, checking anything or fixing your hair.
  2. Each time an urge to move shows up, tap the counter once and notice where it is, like an itch, a restless leg or a pull toward your phone.
  3. Don’t fight the urge and don’t follow it. Watch whether it rises, peaks, shifts or fades.
  4. When the two minutes are up, look at the count, and notice how many of those urges are still there.

The timer needs JavaScript. A phone stopwatch works just as well.

What you’ll probably notice. Sitting still for two minutes can turn up more urges than you’d expect, and most of them crest and fade within seconds, even the itch that felt like it had to be scratched. A bigger urge, like one more game, can rise and fall in a similar way, and watching it closely can make room to choose.

Step by step

Ride the wave, then choose

Start with the urge you know best, like one more game or one more video.

  1. Take your hands off for one minute. When the urge hits, put down the mouse, the phone or the controller. You’re not saying no forever, only not acting for one minute.
  2. Find where it lives. Check your face, jaw, chest, stomach and hands, and name the spot and what it feels like, like heat, a buzz or pressure.
  3. Describe the wave. In your head, say whether it’s rising, peaking, shifting or fading. You don’t have to make it smaller, only watch it change.
  4. Choose after the peak. Wait until the feeling drops, and then pick your next move, with one ready ahead of time, like closing the game, putting the phone in another room or texting a friend.
  5. Keep a count. Make a tally mark for every wave you rode and every one you followed, and over a week, notice whether the peaks pass faster.

How to tell it’s working. For a week, count the urges you rode out and the ones you acted on. If the first column grows, it’s working, even on days when the feeling stays loud.

When it gets hard. The most common slip is fighting, telling yourself “don’t” over and over, which can keep your attention glued to the urge. Switch from arguing to describing, under your breath if that helps.

Good to know. If an urge feels unsafe, keeps taking over your sleep, schoolwork or friendships, or is about vaping, alcohol or another substance, don’t ride it out alone. Bring in a parent or someone else you trust, and a doctor too if a substance is involved.
