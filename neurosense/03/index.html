Why Multitasking Is Slowing You Down

Task Switching & The Cognitive Bottleneck

There's no such thing as parallel attention. Every 'multitask' is rapid switching — and every switch costs you.

The Science

  • Treisman & Gelade (1980): attention must be directed serially when conjunctions of more than one feature are needed. The brain's spotlight is fundamentally one-at-a-time.
  • Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001): every task switch forces the prefrontal cortex to log out of one task and log into another. That switching cost compounds on complex work to ~40% productivity loss.
  • Pashler (1994) — Psychological Refractory Period: when two tasks need the same cognitive resources, the second is delayed until the first is processed.
  • Ward et al. (2017): the mere PRESENCE of a smartphone consumes cognitive resources used to NOT check it. Pocket and face-down both fail.

The Protocol

  • One tab. One task. If you need to look something up, finish the paragraph first.
  • Phone in another room. Pocket and face-down both fail.
  • Batch all notifications into ONE 5-minute window every 90 minutes.
