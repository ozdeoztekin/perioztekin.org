Why Dimming Your Phone May Not Be Enough for Sleep

Evening Room Light & Melatonin

A dimmer phone leaves a bright room's light in place, and light-sensing cells in the eye can respond to both.

The Science

  • Some retinal cells contain melanopsin, a protein that lets them detect light themselves and signal the suprachiasmatic nucleus, your central clock.
  • Through indirect nerve relays, the clock helps regulate melatonin production in the pineal gland, and evening light can reduce that production.
  • Sensitivity varies: earlier pubertal stages can show greater evening melatonin suppression than later stages under the same lighting.

The Protocol

  • Keep homework comfortably lit, and when possible finish the work that needs bright light before your planned wind-down.
  • During wind-down, lower room lighting and screen brightness, keeping enough light to move safely, and put screens away for sleep.
  • If you share a bright room, ask an adult about a dimmer place to settle; get daytime light without looking directly at the sun.

See it in real life

Example

One ceiling light for two brothers

Kasimir shares a bedroom with his older brother, Teodor, who studies most nights until eleven under its bright ceiling light. Kasimir does geometry on his bed, then at ten dims his phone all the way and pulls up the blanket, since a dim screen is supposed to help.

Most nights he’s still awake when the light clicks off, and on Monday that was after midnight. Every “Can you turn it off?” gets “I have a test,” and Teodor insists the light never keeps him up.

The phone was never the only light in that room. Some retinal cells contain melanopsin, a protein that allows them to detect light themselves, and they signal the brain’s central clock. Through relays, the clock helps regulate how much melatonin the pineal gland makes, and evening light, from a ceiling as well as a screen, can reduce it. Sensitivity varies, too. In a lab, young people at earlier stages of puberty showed a bigger melatonin drop under the same evening light than those further along, though their ages overlapped, so the same light may not affect both brothers the same way.

So the fix has to reach past the phone. On Tuesday Kasimir takes the problem to their grandmother, who makes the bedroom the dim place after nine thirty and the kitchen the bright one. His geometry moves to the kitchen table right after dinner, where the light is good for compass work, and at nine thirty Teodor’s books follow. Kasimir switches off the ceiling light, turns on his bedside lamp and, when he’s ready to sleep, puts his phone on the dresser. A plug-in night-light by the door lights Teodor’s way in.

By Friday the geometry has been done before nine every evening, and nobody has asked anybody to turn anything off. Kasimir lies awake a while on Thursday. On the other nights he never hears Teodor come in, though a week can’t show how much of that was the light.

  1. Where it stallsHe dims his phone, but the ceiling light stays on until eleven.
  2. What changes itHomework that needs bright light moves earlier, and the bedroom drops to one lamp.
  3. What happens nextThe arguments about the light stop, and the homework is done before nine.

Try it yourself · 5 minutes

The paper on the pillow

Try this one evening after dark, before your wind-down starts, so it doesn’t push bedtime later. Bring a sheet of white paper and your phone.

  1. In the room where you sleep, turn your phone’s brightness all the way down. From your pillow, count every light you can see, including ceiling lights, lamps, screens and any glow from the hallway.
  2. Stand the paper up against your pillow, facing into the room, about where your eyes would be.
  3. Step back and notice how bright the paper looks with the room lit the way it usually is at that hour.
  4. Turn off the ceiling light, leaving a lamp or the hallway light on so you can move safely, and look at the paper again.
  5. Hold your dimmed phone where you usually hold it in bed, screen toward the paper, and notice how much it adds.

What you’ll probably notice. The paper often stays well lit while the ceiling light is on, even with the phone turned down, and switching off the overhead light usually changes it more than the dimmed phone does. The paper stands in for your eyes, which take in light from the whole room. Some cells in the retina contain melanopsin, so they can detect light themselves and report it to your brain’s central clock, and evening light can lower how much melatonin your body makes. That depends on the light that reaches your eyes, from the room as well as the screen.

Step by step

Dim the room as well as the phone

For evenings when turning your screen down hasn’t been enough, including in a room you share.

  1. Look at the whole room. When your wind-down starts, look past your screen at every light that’s on, like ceiling lights, lamps and the hallway. Your eyes take in all of it, the phone included.
  2. Do the bright-light work first. Keep homework comfortably lit so you can see and work safely. When you can, finish the work that needs bright light before your planned wind-down.
  3. Lower the room and the screen. At wind-down, switch off the bright overhead lights and turn your screen down too. Keep a lamp or a night-light on so you can move around safely.
  4. Put screens away for sleep. When you’re ready to sleep, put your phone and any other screens somewhere off the bed, like a dresser or a charger across the room.
  5. Ask an adult if you share a bright room. If someone in your room needs bright light later than you do, ask a parent or caregiver about a dimmer place to settle. Get ordinary daylight during the day too, without looking straight at the sun, though it isn’t a reliable way to cancel out bright light at night.

How to tell it’s working. For a week, count the lights still on when each wind-down starts, and note whether the bright-light work was already done. Then look at whether the evenings with the work finished early were the ones when the dimmer wind-down was easier to keep.

When it gets hard. The homework that needs bright light is the part that drifts late. If it keeps landing in your wind-down, ask an adult to help you move it to right after school or dinner.

Good to know. Lower melatonin after evening light is a hormone result, not a measure of sleep, and this plan sets no exact brightness or number of minutes. Keep enough light to move safely, never look straight at the sun, and if trouble falling asleep keeps coming back, tell a parent or caregiver so a doctor can check it.
