Power Training Is Not Just Heavier Lifting

Power Training & Movement Velocity

Power needs speed as well as force, so a manageable load moved with fast intent is its own kind of practice.

The Science

  • Motor neurons control groups of muscle fibers, and their thresholds differ: as force demands rise, additional higher-threshold units can be recruited.
  • A heavier weight can build strength while slowing the movement. Power adds velocity: you need force, but you also need to move quickly.
  • A load that barely moves creates a different practice from a manageable load you intend to move fast, and both can be demanding.

The Protocol

  • If you are a trained adult with sound technique, set the load at roughly 40 to 70 percent of an established maximum.
  • On a familiar exercise suited to power work, do 3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 repetitions, resting 3 to 5 minutes between sets.
  • During the lifting phase, intend to move quickly with control; do not learn an unfamiliar lift by moving it explosively.

See it in real life

Example

A beat late off the floor

In Tuesday’s rebounding drill, Ilona boxed out the way she’d been taught, watched the shot go up and was still bending her knees when the other forward’s hands closed on the ball. She came down with two of ten. A sophomore on the college team, she keeps reaching the right spot and losing the ball.

It makes no sense, because she has never been stronger. She’s nineteen, with years of coached lifting behind her, and all summer she added weight to her squat almost every week. Most sets ended with the bar barely moving, and she counted those as her best days.

Each motor neuron controls a group of muscle fibers, together called a motor unit, and the units don’t all join in at the same level of demand. As the force needed rises, more are recruited, including higher-threshold ones, which helps explain why heavy loads build strength. Power adds speed. A load that barely moves is a different practice from a manageable load you intend to move fast, and a rebound needs the fast one, since a slow effort can be strong and still miss the moment.

So the team’s strength coach, Gilbert, keeps her heavy day and makes the other one a power day. She uses the trap-bar deadlift she knows well, at about 55 percent of the maximum she tested with him in August, four sets of three with four minutes of rest, driving each repetition up as fast as she can with control. It feels almost too light, and when she reaches for another plate, Gilbert says speed is the whole job today.

On the last set a repetition comes up slower than she wants, and Gilbert counts it anyway, since she meant it to be fast. She keeps the power day through preseason, and in its final week, in the same drill, she comes down with five of ten, though she can’t tell how much came from the power days and how much from practice.

  1. Where it stallsShe reaches the right spot for rebounds but leaves the floor a beat late.
  2. What changes itOn power days she drives a lighter, familiar lift up fast, with control.
  3. What happens nextIn the last week of preseason she comes down with five of ten.

Try it yourself · 2 minutes

Slow rise, quick bounce

Stand next to a counter or table you can rest a hand on. Nothing here asks for a big jump or your hardest effort.

  1. Rest one palm flat on the counter and wrap your other hand around that forearm. Press down lightly, then a bit harder, and feel what changes under your fingers.
  2. With a hand on the counter for balance, rise onto your toes as slowly as you can, then lower back down. Notice that your feet never leave the floor.
  3. Now do a few small, quick bounces on your toes, like skipping rope, without trying to go high.
  4. Compare the slow rise with the quick bounces. It’s the same body and the same calf muscles both times.

What you’ll probably notice. Your forearm firms up as you press harder, partly because more motor units join in when the force you ask for rises. The slow rise lifts your whole body onto your toes and still leaves you on the floor, while the quick bounce produces its force fast enough to get you off the ground. Power needs force produced quickly, and a heavy bar that barely moves doesn’t practice that part.

Step by step

Lighter load, fast intent

If you’re an adult who already trains with sound technique, ideally with a coach or a team program. This is an expert’s broad framework for power, meant to be adjusted, not a tested program or a workout for everyone.

  1. Make sure the goal is power. Use this when what you need is force produced quickly, like a jump, rather than the most weight you can move.
  2. Choose a familiar lift suited to power work. Pick an exercise you already do with sound technique, and check with your coach that it suits fast lifting. Never learn an unfamiliar lift by moving it explosively.
  3. Load it at roughly 40 to 70 percent. Base it on a maximum you’ve already established, such as one tested with your coach. This isn’t a reason to test a new one.
  4. Keep sets and repetitions low. Do 3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 repetitions, with 3 to 5 minutes of rest between sets, so each set practices speed and doesn’t turn into an endurance grind.
  5. Lift fast, with control. During the lifting phase, intend to move quickly while keeping control. The intention counts even when the bar doesn’t move as fast as you hoped.

How to tell it’s working. Ask your coach to watch or film a set, and note whether every repetition stays quick and controlled. Over several weeks, compare something tied to your goal, like a jump your coach measures, and expect the pace of change to vary.

When it gets hard. The load will feel too light, and you’ll want to add plates. Once the bar slows to a grind, you’re practicing strength rather than power, so keep speed as the target.

Good to know. This isn’t for anyone under 18, for anyone new to lifting or to this exercise (learn the technique first with a qualified coach), or for anyone with an injury or a health condition until a doctor says it’s fine. It’s one training choice for one goal, and it doesn’t mean every exercise should be fast.
