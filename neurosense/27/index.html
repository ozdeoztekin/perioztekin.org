What You Eat Literally Builds Your Brain

Neuro-Nutrition: Fats

Your brain is rich in fat, and some of the most important kinds, the omega-3s, have to come from food.

The Science

  • Bazinet & Layé (Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 2014): the brain is unusually rich in fat, and the omega-3 fatty acid DHA is a major building block of brain cell membranes; EPA, another omega-3, is present in the brain at much lower levels.
  • Bourre (Journal of Nutrition, Health & Aging, 2004): a review of how omega-3 fats support the brain across life; in animal studies, a diet short on omega-3s changed the makeup of brain membranes, including myelin, and impaired learning tasks.
  • Su et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2018): in a meta-analysis of 19 clinical trials with 2,240 participants, omega-3 supplements eased anxiety symptoms on average, but mainly at doses of at least 2,000 mg a day and more clearly in people with clinical diagnoses; mixes with less than 60% EPA worked better than EPA-heavy ones.

The Protocol

  • Eat fatty fish, like salmon, sardines or mackerel, about twice a week.
  • If you don't eat fish, walnuts, chia seeds and flaxseed give you ALA, a plant omega-3, though the body turns only a small share of it into EPA and DHA.
  • Talk with a parent or doctor before trying a fish oil supplement; the doses studied for anxiety were high, and the benefits showed up mainly in people with anxiety diagnoses.
  • Keep it ordinary: these are healthy foods, not a quick fix for focus.
  • Be patient: diet changes act slowly, over weeks to months, not days.

See it in real life

Example

The same step, explained three times

Mr. Duarte holds his phone at arm’s length and asks again where the video button went. It’s the third time this hour, and Elena hears herself sigh. She runs the tech help hour at the community center on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and she likes it, but by 4:30 she’s losing her own place in the steps. All Mr. Duarte wants is to video-call his granddaughter in Lisbon by himself.

Her lunch period is at 10:50, so by four o’clock she hasn’t eaten in hours, and most weeks there’s no fish, nuts or seeds in anything she eats.

Your brain is built partly out of fat. Omega-3 fats, the kind in salmon, sardines, walnuts, chia and flax, are part of the membranes around brain cells, and they’re part of myelin, the fatty coating on nerve fibers that keeps signals moving fast. The body converts only a small share of the plant kind into the forms the brain uses most, so it takes more of it. And because the brain keeps rebuilding these parts, the supply has to keep arriving, with any changes showing up over weeks and months rather than days.

So for three weeks she adds things. She keeps a jar of walnuts in her backpack for the walk to the center. Her dad agrees to salmon tacos on Mondays, she packs a canned salmon sandwich on Thursdays, and she stirs chia into her morning yogurt. Every afternoon she writes three numbers from 0 to 10 in a notebook, for fog, for how steady her mood is and for how hard it is to get started.

By the third week her fog numbers are mostly 4s instead of 7s. She can’t tell how much of that is the omega-3s and how much is finally eating something in the afternoon, but she keeps doing both. On the last Thursday she walks Mr. Duarte through the whole video call without a sigh, and his granddaughter’s face fills the screen.

  1. Where it stallsBy 4:30 she’s foggy and explaining the same step three times.
  2. What changes itShe adds walnuts, salmon and chia, and writes down three numbers a day.
  3. What happens nextBy week three her fog is down, and the video call goes start to finish.

Try it yourself · 5 minutes

The leaky straw

This models what myelin does. You’ll need a drinking straw, scissors, some tape and a paper ball about the size of a pea.

  1. Snip four or five small notches along the middle of the straw, each about the size of a grain of rice.
  2. Set the paper ball on a table, hold the end of the straw right behind it, and blow one short, steady puff. Mark where the ball stops.
  3. Wrap tape around the straw so every notch is sealed.
  4. Put the ball back on the starting spot, blow the same puff and mark where it stops now.

What you’ll probably notice. With the notches open, a lot of your breath leaks out the sides and the ball doesn’t go far, but once they’re sealed, the same puff sends it much farther. Myelin is the fatty insulation around many nerve fibers, and it keeps the signal from leaking away so it travels fast. Omega-3 fats are part of what that insulation and the cell membranes are built from, so the plan below is about a steady supply. A straw isn’t a nerve, though, and no food works as fast as tape.

Step by step

Add it, track it, three weeks

Nothing gets cut. You’re adding one kind of food and watching three numbers.

  1. Add fish twice a week if you can. Salmon, sardines or mackerel all count, fresh or canned, and canned salmon in a sandwich or a taco works fine.
  2. Add a plant source every day. Have a handful of walnuts, or a spoonful of chia or ground flax in yogurt, oatmeal or a smoothie. The body converts only a little of the plant kind, so every day matters.
  3. Keep it steady for three weeks. Steady matters more than big. Give it the full three weeks before you decide anything, and know that the full effect, if there is one, can take longer.
  4. Rate three signals every afternoon. From 0 to 10, rate your afternoon fog, how steady your mood is and how hard it feels to start your work, at the same time each day.

How to tell it’s working. Compare your first week’s numbers with your third, and look at the trend across all three weeks, since a single good or bad day doesn’t tell you much. Sleep, and whether you’ve eaten anything that afternoon, can move these numbers too, so keep those about the same while you test.

When it gets hard. If you don’t like fish, leave it out and have walnuts, chia or flax every day. You can try fish again later in a form you might like better, like salmon in a taco.

Good to know. If you’re allergic to fish or nuts, or your family’s diet rules some of these out, ask a parent or doctor what fits instead. Ask a doctor before starting any supplement.
