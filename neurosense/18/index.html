Memories Are Made While You Sit Still

Hippocampal Consolidation

The hippocampus is what converts short-term experience into long-term memory. Remove it — as happened to patient H.M. — and you can never form a new memory again.

The Science

  • Cox (Harvard MCB80.3x): 'Activity in the hippocampus is necessary for converting short-term memories into long-term memories.'
  • Patient H.M. (Henry Molaison, 1950s): bilateral hippocampectomy for epilepsy → permanent anterograde amnesia. Could remember pre-surgery life but never formed a new declarative memory.
  • Procedural learning (basal ganglia) was intact — H.M. improved at tracing tasks each time, even though he never remembered doing them.
  • Warrington & Shallice (1969): first clean dissociation of short-term from long-term memory in a single patient.

The Protocol

  • 5 minutes of silence after every study block. Phone AWAY, not just face-down.
  • Don't switch immediately to a high-input task. Give the replay window time.
  • Deep sleep amplifies consolidation. All-nighters cost you twice.
