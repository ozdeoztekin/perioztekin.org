Caffeine Doesn't Give You Energy. It Steals Your Off Switch

Neuropharmacology

No drug invents new feelings. Each hijacks an existing neuromodulator system — dopamine, serotonin, acetylcholine, noradrenaline — by mimicking, blocking, or extending what the brain already does.

The Science

  • Harvard MCB80.2x: 'Neuromodulation is the process by which synaptic transmission between two neurons is enhanced or decreased through action of a third substance, the neuromodulator.'
  • 'Most neuromodulators act through G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs)' — they shift synaptic strength rather than directly firing the neuron.
  • Three drug-receptor modes: AGONIST (activates), ANTAGONIST (blocks), REUPTAKE INHIBITOR (lets transmitter linger).
  • Examples: caffeine = adenosine antagonist · nicotine = nicotinic acetylcholine agonist · SSRIs = serotonin reuptake inhibitors.

The Protocol

  • Caffeine timing: wait 90-120 min after waking. Let your natural cortisol cycle do the work first.
  • Audit substances: which receptor? Agonist, antagonist, or reuptake? What's the tolerance curve?
  • Sleep restores receptor sensitivity. Sleep-deprived → everything less effective.
